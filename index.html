01 / OVERVIEW

Local signals.
Accountable human response.

C.H.I.L.D. proposes a distributed child-protection infrastructure combining wearable biosensors, local anomaly detection, minimized event signals and AI–human guardian teams. The framework describes how changes in an individual child’s physiological patterns, movement, proximity and expected presence could inform contextual risk review. It organizes responses through graduated risk states, bounded retention, consent-aware routing and qualified human oversight. Its intended contribution is a technical and governance framework for child abuse prevention, safeguarding intervention and privacy-preserving monitoring. Detection performance, false-positive control and safeguarding outcomes require independent evaluation.

The paper brings sensing, event routing and governance into one specification. Local processing, bounded memory and human oversight are central to its proposed approach.

A physiological anomaly is a reason to examine context, not proof that abuse has occurred.

03 / INDEPENDENT EVALUATION

Questions that deserve careful testing

The proposal invites examination across child welfare, wearable engineering, biosignal processing, clinical research and privacy. Independent evaluation can test whether its mechanisms support safe and useful safeguarding decisions.

  • Detection validity — false positives, missed events and performance across different contexts.
  • Human response — review quality, escalation thresholds and supported decision-making.
  • Privacy and governance — consent, coercion, access controls, retention and independent oversight.
  • Practical outcomes — acceptability, accessibility and effects on children and families.

The specification is research material, not a validated diagnostic tool or an operational emergency service.
